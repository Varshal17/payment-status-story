Our industry answers with Pending

Every customer asks the same three questions about a payment. Where is it? When will it finish? Do I need to do anything? Today the answer changes with the product, the rail and the screen.

This is the case for one simple status system across every payment we offer, and the plan to build it.

Chapter 1

One customer. One week. Five words that mean nothing.

Meet Asha. This week she paid her credit card bill, sent rent to her landlord, bought a coffee and got a refund.

Here is what her activity screen could look like, using words real banks and apps use today.

Illustration. Every label on it comes from the benchmark of 24 banks, fintechs and rails.

Three rows say Pending. They mean three different things.

The coffee is a card hold: nothing to do. The rent is waiting for Ravi to accept: he must act. The refund is under review: the bank is checking it.

Card hold: Monzo. Waiting on recipient: Zelle, Cash App. Review: Revolut, PayPal.

"Done" isn't one moment either.

Her card payment shows Posted. But a bank can post a payment and still hold the credit for days. Capital One says about 3 to 9.

Wise marks a transfer Complete when money reaches the recipient's bank, not their account.

So Asha does what anyone would. She asks.

Every unclear status becomes a support contact, a repeat payment, or a customer who trusts us a little less.

Chapter 2

The best players keep the machinery out of sight.

Payment systems are complicated on purpose. Customers don't need to see it. The leaders run two layers: many states inside, a few plain words outside.

Stripe tracks a payment through seven states. Its dashboard shows only five labels.

Three different system states all read "Incomplete" to the person looking.

Stripe, payment status docs.

Wise does the same for customers. Ten states inside its API. Eight plain labels outside, like "On its way to Wise".

Adyen runs about sixty. Nobody shows sixty.

Wise transfer statuses. Adyen payment lifecycle.

Now look at the market as a whole. We found about thirty words for status across banks, fintechs and rails.

Each is someone's honest attempt. Together, they're noise.

Sort them by what they actually mean, and only seven situations are left.

One row is almost empty. No one has a good word for "you need to act." That's the moment where silence costs customers money.

Those seven become our system. One state each, one fixed label each, on every product and every screen.

The detail moves into the sentence under the label, where it can be specific.

Chapter 4

One event. The same words on every screen.

Asha's UPI transfer is running late. Watch one status event travel to every place she might look.

The rail reports one fact: the receiving bank hasn't confirmed yet. Our system turns it into a state, a cause and a deadline.

Everything she sees is written once, as a content record, and pulled from there.

In the app, the chip says In progress, in amber because it's late. The sentence says who has the money and when it ends.

The push and the SMS say the same thing, shorter. The SMS carries no link, so it can't be mistaken for phishing.

The AI agent reads the same record through one tool. It may rephrase. It may not promise a date we can't keep, or tell her to pay again.

Support sees what she sees, plus the raw rail code. No more "the app says one thing and the agent says another."

Chapter 6

The strategy, in four choices.

Each choice is small. Together they turn status from a copy problem in every team into one shared system.

One model for every product

Seven states, fixed labels, the same on cards, bill pay, transfers and every rail we add.

Why: the benchmark found about 30 words for 7 situations. Consistency is the fix customers feel first.

Words are data, not code

Every message is a content record with an ID. Screens, comms and the agent pull from it. Change it once, it changes everywhere.

Why: it ends the push that says "done" while the app says "pending".

Rails map in, words stay put

A new payment method adds mapping rows. It gets new copy only when a reviewed decision says it needs it.

Why: new rails ship in days of mapping, not weeks of writing and review.

The agent says what the app says

AI answers come from the same status and the same words, with a list of things it must never claim.

Why: an agent that improvises status is a regulatory and trust risk.

Chapter 7

The action plan.

Four phases, each with a clear exit. We start where we own the most and where the risk is highest: card bill payments. Timings get set with engineering in phase one.

  1. 1Agree

    Lock the foundation

    • Approve the seven states, labels and vocabulary.
    • Accept the five decision records from the pressure test.
    • Name owners for words, state logic and the event pipeline.
    • Compliance reviews the regulatory hooks: Reg Z, Reg E, RBI turnaround times.
    Done whenEvery decision record has a named decider, and counsel has signed each regulatory hook.
  2. 2Pilot

    Card bill payments, three surfaces

    • Build the status event and the content resolver.
    • Write the content records for bill pay, with safe defaults for all seven states.
    • Ship to the app, push notifications and the AI agent. The agent is the strictest test.
    • Measure today's status-related contacts as the baseline.
    Done whenEvery bill-pay status renders from one record, with the same words on all three surfaces, and the baseline is in hand.
  3. 3Scale

    Every rail, every surface

    • Map card purchases, ACH, UPI, BBPS and wires.
    • Add email, SMS, statements, voice and the support console.
    • Retire screen-by-screen status copy as each journey moves over.
    Done whenFewer than 1% of rail codes fall back to "unmapped", and fewer than 5% of messages use the generic default.
  4. 4Run

    Keep it simple as we grow

    • Fast path for routine changes, review gates for big ones.
    • Monthly reports on unmapped codes and the records driving most contacts.
    • Quarterly vocabulary review against complaint data.
    Done whenA new rail ships through mapping alone, with no new copy.
